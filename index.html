How we see flares

VIIRS satellites (Suomi NPP, NOAA-20/21) record heat sources on the ground every night. A wildfire lasts a few days and moves; a flare burns in the same spot for months. We take the detections for the chosen window, put them on a 0.015° grid (~1.5 km) and keep the cells that light up on at least 35% of nights.

How we estimate volume

The satellite measures radiated power (FRP, MW). Radiation is roughly a quarter of a flare's total heat, so thermal power = FRP ÷ 0.25. Dividing by the heating value of 36 MJ/m³ gives m³ per second, then m³ per year adjusted for persistence. This is a screening estimate; the operator's flow meter is what calibrates it.

Money and CO₂

Methanol: 1,100 m³ of gas per tonne, ₸250,000 per tonne (import parity). Electricity: 3.5 kWh per m³ at ₸25 per kWh. CO₂-eq: 2.57 kg per m³ from the World Bank report (167 bcm → 429 Mt in 2025). All prices are our assumptions and live in app/economics.py.
